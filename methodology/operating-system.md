# Reusable AI-assisted partnerships operating system

## Core proposition

**A reusable, AI-assisted partnerships operating system, demonstrated through a focused Sovrano market case.**

The operating system is a repeatable commercial method for converting public evidence into prioritised partnership hypotheses, bounded propositions, testable campaigns and reviewed outputs. It is not a deployed software product, autonomous agent or claim of proprietary technology.

## The seven operating layers

| Layer | Reusable method | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Evidence | Capture a precise public signal, official source, verification date and claim boundary. | Auditable source ledger. |
| 2. Prioritisation | Score need, timing, strategic fit, expansion, access and delivery feasibility. | Ranked account radar with visible assumptions. |
| 3. Proposition | Convert the signal into a labelled hypothesis and bounded first offer. | Buyer problem, partnership wedge, pilot and expansion logic. |
| 4. Activation | Convert public research or a benchmark signal into useful, evidence-bounded outreach and discovery. | Campaign architecture, EuroExec activation path, metrics and decision rules. |
| 5. Forecasting | Translate buyer-confirmed stages into a 90-day commercial view and low/base/high expert-capacity signal. | Stage evidence, next actions, demand range and forecast confidence. |
| 6. Evaluation | Compare unconstrained and evidence-constrained AI outputs. | Prompts, model outputs, weighted rubric and adjudication notes. |
| 7. Governance | Apply human review, privacy, affiliation, delivery and publication controls. | Public/private boundary, disclaimer and release checklist. |

## Reusable versus case-specific

| Reusable across future projects | Replaced for each new case |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence schema and claim boundaries | Target company, market and role brief |
| Scoring criteria and tier logic | Named accounts and current market signals |
| Bounded-pilot architecture | Buyer problems, propositions and entry offers |
| Campaign experiment design | Message language and channel assumptions |
| Stage evidence and forecast schema | Illustrative weights, actual pipeline and capacity assumptions |
| AI evaluation rubric | Scenario sources, prompts and outputs |
| Human-review and publication controls | Case-specific legal, delivery and privacy risks |

## Error register

| Risk | How it enters | Control | Residual limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source error | Public information is incomplete, stale or misunderstood. | Official links, verification dates and short claim boundaries. | Public evidence cannot reveal internal strategy or procurement intent. |
| Inference error | A relevant signal is converted into an assumed need. | Fact/hypothesis separation and conditional language. | Commercial judgement remains subjective. |
| Model error | AI omits, distorts or invents detail. | Evidence-constrained prompts, rubric scoring and critical-failure rules. | No prompt eliminates model error; human review remains mandatory. |
| Scoring bias | Famous accounts or preferred ideas receive inflated ratings. | Transparent weights, editable scores and evidence checks. | Scores are prioritisation aids, not probabilities. |
| Delivery error | An illustrative pilot implies unverified capability or access. | Bounded scope, dependencies, consent, rights and safety checks. | Real delivery requires company-specific discovery and approval. |
| Publication error | A simulation appears to be real activity, affiliation or results. | Clear labels, public/private separation and pre-publication checklist. | Context can still be lost when excerpts are shared separately. |
| Freshness error | Company priorities, roles or sources change. | Re-verification before every application, interview or publication. | The repository is a dated snapshot, not live market intelligence. |

## Human decision rights

AI accelerates research structuring, drafting, comparison and quality checks. The human owner remains accountable for:

- deciding which sources are credible;
- distinguishing evidence from interpretation;
- changing weights and scores;
- validating delivery capability;
- approving external language and publication;
- deciding whether any hypothesis merits real outreach.

## Applying the system to another project

1. Replace the case-specific brief, market and company set.
2. Rebuild the source ledger from current official evidence.
3. Re-score accounts rather than carrying ratings forward.
4. Redesign entry offers around the new organisation's actual public proposition.
5. Run fresh evaluation scenarios and preserve first-pass outputs.
6. Apply the [reusable disclosure template](disclosure-template.md) before publication.

The Sovrano case demonstrates the method; it does not validate every future use of the system.
